# Client satisfaction

Open a job's **Handover** section to work with satisfaction surveys. A client chooses email in **Survey settings**; the builder cannot give consent for them. An unsubscribe stops these optional messages. Current job access and feature permissions still apply.

The owner configures a title, one to eight questions, a low-score threshold and an HTTPS Google Business Profile or Houzz review link in **Survey settings**. Scores run from 1 to 10. Each invitation retains the settings, source and people involved when it was prepared, so later settings changes cannot rewrite a response.

After builder completion and client acceptance of a handover, or after a trade submits original warranty-visit evidence, the owner can send one survey to each consenting assigned client. Choose a currently authorized office person to own any low-score follow-up. A low response creates an assigned checklist in **To-dos**, without sending another message. If that person has lost access before the response arrives, the active installation owner receives the follow-up.

Clients submit their own immutable score, answers and optional comment. Office users see the job's responses and follow-ups. The Jobs page's **Satisfaction by manager and trade** report groups responses by the project managers, crew and subcontractors involved at invitation time, under the viewer's current job grants. Saved job views also offer response count, average satisfaction and low-score count columns.

Public review permission is separate from survey consent and can be withdrawn before an invitation. Every score is eligible. Only an owner explicitly sends a permitted review invitation, using the review link frozen with that survey. Plinth does not collect, edit, filter or publish the public review.

A reserved send attempt is durable. Provider acceptance does not establish delivery. Refused, unconfigured, development and unknown outcomes stay distinct; none is automatically retried. Private archives preserve survey history and assigned checklists. Restored surveys remain historical and cannot authorize new messages; the receiving installation needs fresh client consent and a new survey. Erasing a job removes its surveys and prevents completion or replay from recreating it.
